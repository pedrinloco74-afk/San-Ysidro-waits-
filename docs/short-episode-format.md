# San Ysidro Waits — 15–30 Second Episode Format

A fast, warm animated micro-series about Chuy moving between Tijuana and San Ysidro. The joke is never the people or their identity; it is the impossible logic of waiting.

## The repeatable shape

**Target length:** 20–24 seconds for the first batch. The same structure can stretch to 15 or 30 seconds.

| Time | Beat | Animation goal |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:03 | **Hook** — an instantly recognizable Tijuana problem | One strong pose, sign, sound, or location detail |
| 0:03–0:08 | **Complication** — the line, a rule, or a tiny misunderstanding gets worse | Two or three reusable reaction poses |
| 0:08–0:16 | **Chuy's big idea** — Spanglish Boost and street knowledge make the problem feel solvable | Fast cuts, graphic transitions, one fantasy accent |
| 0:16–0:21 | **The turn** — Chuy learns the line cannot actually be shortened | Hold for the laugh or emotional beat |
| 0:21–0:24 | **Button** — a final phrase, Semáforo bark, or DON ESPERA reaction | End on a clean loopable image |

## Production rules for limited animation

- Build each short from 5–8 shots, with 2–4 key poses per character.
- Keep Chuy's **TJ-only green cap**, red brim, rosary, orange-and-black jersey, xolo socks, unbranded shoes, bare wrists, and empty pockets consistent in every shot.
- Use camera moves, mouth swaps, eye darts, hand accents, and background pans instead of full-body animation whenever possible.
- Let Tijuana do visual work: minibus colors, taco steam, dusty ochre walls, Pacific-blue distance, a corner shop, a traffic light, or a familiar street rhythm. Do not stop for tourist explanations.
- No official papers or passports in a character's hand. Chuy's citizenship can be spoken about, but it stays in the coffee tin at Doña Cuquita's house.
- Spanish and English should switch naturally. Do not translate every line; the rhythm is the joke.
- Sound design carries scale: distant traffic, a vendor call, footsteps, a bus brake, the border-line murmur, and Semáforo's one meaningful bark.

## Episode 01 — “Mango Express”

**Runtime:** 22 seconds  
**Location:** Cañón Flores Magón, Tijuana → the pedestrian line toward San Ysidro  
**Cast:** Chuy, Doña Cuquita (voice), Semáforo (voice), two waiting neighbors  
**Core problem:** Chuy is late with a cup of mango con chile for Mamá Ruth.  
**Rule demonstrated:** Chuy can make waiting bearable, never shorter.

### Timed script / storyboard

**0:00–0:03 — HOOK**  
Close on a plastic cup: mango, lime, and chile wobble dangerously. Tijuana morning sounds. Chuy enters frame at a run.

**DOÑA CUQUITA (off):** “¡Chuy! El mango para tu mamá!”

**0:03–0:07 — COMPLICATION**  
Chuy skids to the end of the line. It snakes out of frame. He sniffs once; a tiny green pulse travels down the queue.

**CHUY:** “Cronómetro says… forty minutes.”

**0:07–0:12 — BIG IDEA**  
Chuy pivots to the waiting neighbors, switching languages as he moves. The line becomes a tiny rhythmic percussion ensemble: shoes tap, a bag crinkles, someone claps twice.

**CHUY:** “Okay, people, no empujen. We make it a concert.”

**0:12–0:17 — FANTASY ACCENT**  
Quick montage: Chuy conducts with the lime spoon; a wall, a bus window, and a bright Tijuana sign slide by like a stage backdrop. DON ESPERA briefly appears as a puff of gray exhaust, covering his ears.

**NEIGHBOR:** “¿Y la fila?”

**CHUY:** “Still the same fila.”

**0:17–0:20 — TURN**  
The line advances one step. Everyone celebrates as if they crossed a finish line. The mango cup wobbles; Chuy catches it without stopping.

**0:20–0:22 — BUTTON**  
Semáforo barks once. Cut to the traffic light: red. Chuy looks at camera.

**CHUY:** “A horita, we dance.”

End on the same foot-tap rhythm used under the opening. The short can loop cleanly.

## Batch ideas

1. **Mango Express** — the pilot above.
2. **A Horita** — Don Espera repeats “a horita” until the whole line freezes; Chuy breaks the spell by making everyone finish the sentence together.
3. **The Blue Minibus** — Chuy predicts the exact bus that will arrive, but Semáforo only barks at the red one.
4. **$1.40** — a coin rolls through the line; three neighbors turn a rescue into a relay race.
5. **Rain in Tijuana** — one umbrella becomes a moving roof for six people, but nobody agrees whose turn it is to hold it.
6. **Two Lunches** — Chuy carries a lunch from San Ysidro to Tijuana and discovers it has crossed more borders than he has that day.

## Animation handoff checklist

- [ ] Confirm runtime and frame rate before layout.
- [ ] Make a 6–8 panel storyboard before recording final dialogue.
- [ ] Record dialogue first; animate mouth shapes to the performance.
- [ ] Create reusable Chuy, Doña Cuquita, Semáforo, queue, and Tijuana street rigs.
- [ ] Export a vertical 9:16 cut and a horizontal 16:9 cut from the same action where possible.
- [ ] Add burned-in English captions while preserving the Spanish/Spanglish audio rhythm.
- [ ] Keep each episode self-contained: one small problem, one inventive solution, one final button.
