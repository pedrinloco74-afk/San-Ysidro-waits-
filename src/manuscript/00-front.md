# READ THIS FIRST

:::truth THE SHORT VERSION
You are not crazy. You are not paranoid. You are not "too much."

Something changed. You noticed it before you could name it. And no matter how many times you told yourself it was nothing — it kept happening.

This book is going to hand you a plan.
:::

Most people who suspect a partner is cheating do one of two things.

They either **explode** — corner them, accuse them, start a fight at 11 p.m. on a Tuesday — and then spend the next three months apologizing for "being insecure" while the real problem gets buried deeper.

Or they **rot**. They sit on it. They refresh a screen forty times a day. They lose eleven pounds and tell people it was stress. They wait for a confession that is never coming, because a confession is the one thing a cheater will hand you last.

Either way, the result is the same: you end up with pain but no proof, and a partner who has now been warned.

This book is the third option.

:::keypoints WHAT YOU'RE HOLDING
- A **suspicion system**. How to tell a real signal from your own anxiety — in about ten minutes.
- The **25 signs** that actually matter, ranked by how hard they are to fake.
- The **Seven Rules** that keep you from blowing up your own case in week one.
- The **Legal Line**. Exactly where investigation ends and a criminal charge begins — because nothing destroys your position faster than evidence you weren't allowed to collect.
- The **six-part playbook**: phones, money, time, the internet, paperwork, and people.
- The **14-Day Proof Plan**. A day-by-day schedule you can start tonight.
- The **confrontation script** — word for word.
- What they will say, and what to say back.
- The **stay-or-go decision**, made with a clear head instead of a broken heart.
- The **Red Flag Scorecard**, the **Evidence Log**, and the **Cheater's Dictionary** — the 25 lines you'll hear and what each one actually means.
:::

:::note HOW TO READ THIS
This is a workbook, not a novel. You do not have to read it front to back. Start with **Chapter 2** to score what you're seeing. Then go straight to **Part Three** for the methods that fit your life.

Keep a notebook. Underline things. Write the dates down. Six weeks from now, when you're exhausted and someone is telling you it never happened, your own handwriting will be the thing that keeps you sane.
:::

:::legal NOT LEGAL ADVICE, NOT THERAPY
This book is education, not a substitute for a licensed attorney, therapist, or private investigator.

Surveillance, recording, and privacy law varies wildly from state to state and country to country, and it changes. Before you collect anything, confirm what is legal where you live — and if a divorce is anywhere on your horizon, ask a family-law attorney *before* you start, not after. Evidence gathered illegally is often inadmissible, and in some places collecting it is itself a crime.

If you are afraid of your partner — if there has been any threat, any violence, any control of your money, your phone, or your movements — stop investigating and start with **Chapter 18**. Your safety outranks the truth. Every time.
:::
