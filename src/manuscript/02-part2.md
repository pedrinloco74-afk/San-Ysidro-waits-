# PART TWO
## The Rules of the Hunt

# Chapter 4: The Seven Rules

Every failed investigation fails for the same seven reasons, and every one of them is avoidable. Learn these before you touch a single thing.

## Rule 1 — Do Not Confront Early

The urge will be overwhelming. You'll want to say *"I know"* and watch their face. Resist it.

The moment you confront, four things happen at once: they learn what you know, they get to see your evidence before you organize it, they get time to build a story, and you get nothing new — because your only card is now face up on the table.

Wait until you have either **proof** or **a decision**. If you have proof, confrontation is a conversation about consequences. If you have only a decision, then you don't need a confession at all — and knowing that is the most powerful position in this entire book.

## Rule 2 — Act Boring

You need one week — ideally two — of being completely ordinary. No cold shoulders, no interrogation at dinner, no "who was that?"

Give them no signal. A calm, normal partner is a partner who gets comfortable, and comfortable people get sloppy.

## Rule 3 — Write It Down the Same Day

Memory is a terrible witness. It rewrites itself to fit the story you tell your friends, and it collapses under any pressure.

Every odd thing: date, time, exact words, what you observed, and what you did afterwards. Even the little ones. Especially the little ones.

## Rule 4 — Keep It Private

Every single person you tell is a potential leak, and a "helpful" friend who corners your partner at a barbecue will destroy two weeks of quiet work in one night.

Tell one person. Someone steady, someone who doesn't gossip, someone who won't go rogue. If you tell nobody, that's a legitimate choice too — you have this book, and there's a whole chapter on support in Chapter 18.

## Rule 5 — Do Not Detonate Your Own House

Your identity here is a partner with questions. Not a prosecutor, not a spy, not someone who "ruined the relationship by snooping."

That means: no yelling across a full restaurant, no posting online, no calling their mother at 1 a.m., no confronting the other person at their job, no revenge, no threats, no drama that you'd be ashamed to describe in court. Which brings us to the part that decides cases.

## Rule 6 — Stay On the Legal Side of the Line

This rule has its own chapter (Chapter 5) because it is the rule that most often turns the wronged partner into the defendant.

Two sentences to hold in your head at all times:

**Illegal evidence is thrown out, and can be used against you.**
**An accusation that goes public before it goes private can cost you your job, your kids, and your credibility.**

## Rule 7 — Look After the Detective

You are running a case on four hours of sleep with a nervous system set to alarm. That's not a moral failing; that's a depleted human.

Eat something real every day. Sleep on your side of the bed like a professional. Move your body. Drink water before you drink wine. Vent to your one person, not to a group chat. And if the days start blurring, talk to somebody qualified — see Chapter 18.

:::truth WHY THE RULES EXIST
You are not trying to win an argument. You are trying to *know*, and to still respect yourself afterwards.
:::

# Chapter 5: The Legal Line

This is the chapter that protects you. Read it twice.

You are allowed to look at things that are yours, things you're openly allowed to see, and things that are public. You are generally **not** allowed to break into, hack, intercept, or secretly track anyone — including your own spouse. Marriage does not create an exception to privacy law, and "we're married" is not a defense.

:::legal THE FOUR FELONY-SHAPED MISTAKES
**1. Hidden spyware or tracking software on their phone.** Installing surveillance software (or a hidden tracking app) on an adult's phone without their consent is illegal in most jurisdictions, and in many it's a criminal offense, not a "relationship issue."

**2. GPS trackers on their vehicle.** Legal in some places, illegal in others, and often illegal when the car is theirs alone or when a protective order exists. Where it's permitted, it's usually permitted on a vehicle you own. Check your law *before*, not after.

**3. Recording a conversation or a phone call they're part of.** Some places allow it if one party consents (you), others require *everyone* to consent. Get it wrong and you've committed a crime — and the recording becomes evidence against you.

**4. Reading their email, cloud account, or DMs without authorization.** Accessing an account without permission can violate computer-crime and privacy statutes even when you know the password. Yes, even if you set up the account. Yes, even if you pay for the phone plan.
:::

A hard truth for the do-it-yourself investigator: **a closed case won't help you if it was closed badly.** Courts care how evidence was obtained. Judges have seen every trick, and a partner who broke a law to get a screenshot will spend the rest of the case explaining that instead of the affair.

:::donow THE FIVE QUESTIONS BEFORE YOU COLLECT ANYTHING
1. **Whose property is this?** Mine, ours, or theirs?
2. **Where was the expectation of privacy?** A shared laptop on the kitchen counter and a locked phone are different objects.
3. **Am I allowed to see this by law here —** not by my feelings?
4. **Would this survive the light of day** if I had to hand it to a lawyer and explain how I got it?
5. **Who else could this hurt?** Kids, a coworker, a stranger with rights.
:::

## What Is Safe — Almost Everywhere

- **Anything public.** Public posts, public profiles, public comments, public records, public listings, published check-ins and tags.
- **Anything that is jointly yours.** Your shared bank statement, your shared phone bill, your shared cloud folder, your shared calendar — the items that are set up in both your names and that you're an authorized user of.
- **Your own home.** What you see with your own eyes in your own house, what you find in shared spaces, the state of your own shared bedroom.
- **Your own records.** Your phone records, your calendars, your receipts, your home security footage covering your own home.
- **Things they hand you.** A phone in your hand with permission, a laptop they ask you to check, an unlocked device they leave with you. Keep in mind: consent can be withdrawn later, so ask yourself how it will look.
- **Public records.** Property records, marriage and divorce filings, court records, business registrations, professional licenses, and the sex-offender registry all live online and are free.

## What Is "Ask a Lawyer First"

- Shared cloud accounts that they also use (a family iCloud, a shared Google account, a "family" Netflix — these are murky, not free).
- Family devices used by both of you and by your kids.
- Cameras inside your own home where other people — guests, a nanny, a tenant — might be recorded.
- Anything involving their employer, their work email, or their work property.

## What Is Never Worth It

Breaking into an account. Installing spyware. Cloning a phone. Hiding a tracker. Posing as them. Calling a phone company and impersonating your spouse. Contacting the other person's employer or spouse out of anger. Publishing private photos — which can be a crime and is, without exception, a terrible idea.

:::watch THE REAL COST
Every illegal method has three costs, and people count only one of them:

**The legal cost.** Charges, protective orders, custody consequences, a protective order that *they* get against *you*.

**The evidentiary cost.** The proof is excluded, and now the judge thinks you're the dangerous one.

**The moral cost.** You have to live with what you did for the rest of your life, long after you've stopped caring about them.
:::

:::truth THE GOOD NEWS
The legal methods work better anyway.

Cheaters don't get caught by spyware. They get caught by **patterns, paperwork, and their own mouths.** The next eight chapters are full of both.
:::

# Chapter 6: Building the Case File

You're going to build what a professional would build. Not because you're going to trial — because **the file is what keeps you sane and the file is what makes them stop lying.**

A case file does three things: it converts chaos into sequence, it makes your memory offloadable, and it turns a heap of small oddities into one undeniable shape.

## Your Set-Up

:::donow BUILD IT TONIGHT (20 MINUTES)
1. **One private folder.** Not on a shared device. Your own phone, your own cloud account, or a cheap notebook. Encrypted if it's digital.
2. **One log.** An **Evidence Log** looks like this and you'll find a printable version in Appendix B:
   - Date and time observed
   - What exactly happened (facts only)
   - What they said, in their exact words, in quotation marks
   - What I have to show it (photo, screenshot, receipt, name of witness)
   - My reaction (did I confront, did I ask, did I say nothing)
3. **One screenshots folder**, dated in the filename: `2026-04-11_uber-receipt.png`.
4. **One backup.** Cloud plus your own device. Never only one copy — never only on the device they could reach.
5. **One password they don't know.** New one. Unique. Right now.
:::

## The Six-Column Discipline

Every entry answers six questions. Get these right, and your file will be worth more than any recording you could have made illegally.

:::table THE EVIDENCE LOG FORMAT
**1. WHEN** | Date and time. Vague entries are worthless.
**2. WHAT** | What you observed with your own eyes or ears. No interpretation.
**3. WORDS** | Their exact words in quotes. Delete every adjective you added.
**4. ARTIFACT** | What exists to support it — receipt, photo, name, screenshot, witness.
**5. SOURCE** | Where it came from: "joint card statement," "kitchen counter," "public post."
**6. IMPACT** | One line on what it means — and a separate line if it fits other evidence.
:::

## Two Rules About Strength

**Weak evidence stacks.** One unexplained charge is nothing. Four, on the same four Thursdays, in the same suburb, next to the same name, is a position you can stand on.

**Write down the innocent explanation too.** Force yourself to fill in the column: *What else could explain this?* A file that anticipates the defense is a file nobody can puncture — including you, at 3 a.m., when you start doubting yourself.

:::truth THE EFFORT TEST
If it took effort to hide it, it wasn't innocent.

Nobody deletes a photo album twice a week and changes their passcode monthly by accident.
:::
