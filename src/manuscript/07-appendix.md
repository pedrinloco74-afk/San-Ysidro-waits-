# APPENDIX A
## The Toolkit

Everything in this appendix is designed to be written in, photographed, or printed. Copy it, fill it in, and keep it where your partner cannot reach it.

# Appendix A1: The Red Flag Scorecard

Score each line from 0 to 3. Three months of history. Be stingy. Enter your total at the bottom.

:::table SCORECARD WORKSHEET
**ITEMS** | **SCORE 0–3**

New or changed password, PIN, or screen lock |
Second phone, hidden app, or contact saved under a fake name |
Messages or call history routinely cleared |
Sleep or work schedule no longer matches the shared calendar |
Unexplained out-of-house time of two hours or more per week |
Story details changed when retold |
Angry or cold when asked ordinary questions about the day |
Physical affection at home dropped noticeably |
Sexual interest changed (up or down) without explanation |
Grooming or wardrobe change with no interest in you noticing |
New financial mystery: cash, unexplained charges, hidden account |
Public timeline mismatch — a post or check-in that contradicts you |
Deflects, jokes, or lectures when the relationship is raised |
Reassurance that never comes with specific information |
Your own sleep or appetite disrupted for two weeks or more |
Someone with nothing to gain told you something they saw |
:::

**TOTAL SCORE: ____________ / 48**

:::table WHAT YOUR TOTAL MEANS
**0–9** | Most likely noise. Something is off, but probably not infidelity. Use Chapter 12 and re-check in 30 days.
**10–19** | A real pattern. Move to the Seven Rules, start the Evidence Log, run the 14-Day Plan.
**20+** | Investigate now, carefully. Do not confront until you have proof or a decision.
:::

# Appendix A2: The Evidence Log

Print ten copies, or copy this table into a notebook. One row per event. Facts only.

:::table EVIDENCE LOG (COPY AS MANY AS YOU NEED)
**WHEN** | **WHAT HAPPENED (facts only)** | **THEIR EXACT WORDS** | **ARTIFACT / SOURCE** | **INNOCENT EXPLANATION?** | **NOTES**
         |                             |                        |                      |                            |
         |                             |                        |                      |                            |
         |                             |                        |                      |                            |
         |                             |                        |                      |                            |
         |                             |                        |                      |                            |
         |                             |                        |                      |                            |
:::

Reminders that keep a log honest:

- **No adjectives.** "He was late" is a fact. "He was clearly lying" is a verdict.
- **Quote them.** Exact words, in quotation marks, while you still remember the rhythm.
- **Date everything.** An undated entry is a story about a feeling.
- **Write the innocent explanation too.** A log that anticipates the defense can't be broken by you at 3 a.m. or by them at mediation.

# Appendix B: Scripts You Can Copy

Five moments. Word for word. Adjust the names, keep the structure.

:::script 1 · THE CALM CHECK-IN
> "Hey. I've felt like something has been off between us for about six weeks. I'm not accusing you of anything. I just want to know what's going on with you."

Then stop talking. Count to fifteen in your head. Let them fill the silence.
:::

:::script 2 · THE CONFRONTATION OPENER
> "I need to talk to you about something, and I'm not here to fight. On March 12th you told me you were at the gym until nine. The phone account shows the navigation to Fairview, and the card shows dinner for two at 8:40. I've been living with a pit in my stomach for a month and I can't do it anymore. I want to hear it from you. What's going on?"
:::

:::script 3 · THE DENIAL RESPONSE
> "I hear you saying nothing happened. I'm not going to argue with you about it. I just want to say out loud what I know: I have a repeating pattern of evidence, and your explanation doesn't cover it. I'm going to take three days to think. I'd like you to think too."
:::

:::script 4 · THE "I NEED SPACE" RESPONSE
> "Yes. You can have space. I need two things first: a date when we're talking again, and no more contact with her. If that's a problem, tell me now, because that's what I'll be deciding on."
:::

:::script 5 · TELLING PEOPLE
> "There's something going on in my marriage and I'm not ready to go into details yet. What I need from you right now is company and no questions. I'll tell you the rest when I know it."

Use that with your whole circle. It buys you weeks of peace and it never gets quoted back at you.
:::

:::script 6 · TELLING THE KIDS (AGE-APPROPRIATE, TOGETHER)
> "We want to tell you something, and we want you to know two things first: you did nothing wrong, and we both love you completely. Mom and Dad are having a hard time in our relationship and we're going to get help to decide what happens next. Whatever happens, you have both of us."

And then never say another word about it that the kids didn't ask for. Not once.
:::

# Appendix C: Resources at 3 A.M.

National and international help lines, support communities, and the professional who is worth the money.

:::keypoints CRISIS AND SUPPORT
- **United States — Domestic Violence Hotline:** 1-800-799-7233 (TTY 1-800-787-3224). Text START to 88788. 24/7, free, confidential.
- **United States — 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline:** call or text **988**, 24/7.
- **Outside the U.S.:** search *"domestic violence hotline"* plus your country; most have one free national number.
- **National sexual assault hotlines** exist separately if you need one — search the same way.
:::

:::keypoints PROFESSIONALS WORTH THE MONEY
- **Family-law attorney:** one paid consultation. Ask about evidence, recording laws, joint accounts, and what your options actually are. You don't have to file to benefit.
- **Licensed couples/individual therapist:** specifically ask whether they treat **infidelity**. Ask how many couples they've taken through disclosure, and whether they do structured, full-disclosure work. The generalists are cheaper, and they're why people say "therapy didn't work."
- **Certified divorce/family mediator:** cheaper and faster than court, and often kinder. Good for people who already agree.
- **Forensic accountant:** only if the money has been hidden, and only when the numbers are large enough to justify it.
- **Private investigator (licensed):** legal, expensive, and worth it only in specific cases; make sure they are licensed in your state and ask exactly what evidence they'd document and how.
:::

:::watch THREE ONLINE SAFETY RULES
- **Clear your history** — or better, use your own phone and your own private window and don't leave saved logins behind.
- **Use a private email** they can't open for anything sensitive, and turn off shared-family notification and location sharing you don't want them seeing.
- **Never** post what you're feeling right now. Do not. Not once. Put it in the notebook, in this book, or in a message to your one person.
:::
